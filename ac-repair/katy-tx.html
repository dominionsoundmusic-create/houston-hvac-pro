If your outdoor unit has ever been in floodwater

Katy has a flood history, and properties in and around the Barker and Addicks reservoir pools carry a particular HVAC question that most of the country never has to ask.

A condenser that has been partly or fully submerged is not automatically ruined, and it is not automatically fine either. The electrical components are the issue, not the coil. Contactors, capacitors, the compressor terminals and the disconnect box are where water and silt cause trouble, sometimes months later.

What was submergedWhat it usually means
Water reached the base but not the electrical panelOften recoverable. Coil needs flushing of silt, wiring inspected
Water reached the electrical compartmentComponents should be replaced, not dried out and hoped for
Full submersion including the compressorUsually replacement. Water inside a sealed system is not repairable

If your unit went underwater and has been running since without being checked, get it inspected before summer rather than during a failure. Silt in the coil fins is invisible from the outside and quietly strangles heat rejection.

Raising the condenser

If you are replacing a system on a property that has flooded, ask about mounting the new condenser on a raised platform. It costs modestly on an install that is happening anyway and removes the entire problem for the life of the equipment. Many Katy homeowners do this after the first time and wish they had done it before.

New construction has its own faults

Katy is still building, and a system in its first few years fails differently from an old one. What turns up on newer houses:

  • Ducts disconnected during construction and never noticed, so one room never cools properly from day one.
  • Condensate lines with insufficient slope, which drain poorly and block early.
  • Systems sized by square footage rather than by load calculation, which in a new tight house often means oversized — short cycling, cold and clammy rather than cold and dry.

If your house is new and one room has never been right, that is a construction defect rather than a service issue, and it may still be within builder warranty. Worth checking before you pay anyone.

Before you call

  • Say whether the property has flooded and whether the outdoor unit was in the water.
  • If the house is under about three years old, check the builder warranty first.
  • Note which rooms are affected — one room or the whole house is a different diagnosis.
  • Check the filter and both breakers.
