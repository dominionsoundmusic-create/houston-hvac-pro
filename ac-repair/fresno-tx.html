A new house is not a trouble-free house

Fresno, Texas has grown quickly, and most of its housing is recent. New equipment fails differently from old equipment, and in a fast-built subdivision the faults cluster in a recognisable way.

  • Ducts disconnected during construction. A flex run pulls off a boot or plenum, nobody notices, and one room has never cooled properly since the day you moved in.
  • Condensate lines with too little slope. They drain poorly, block early, and the float switch shuts the system down in the first or second summer.
  • Systems sized by square footage rather than a load calculation. In a tight new house that usually means oversized, which produces a cold, clammy house rather than a comfortable one.
  • Airflow left at the factory default rather than set to the installed system's specification.

Check your builder warranty before paying anyone. If a room has never cooled properly in a house under about two years old, that is a construction defect rather than a service call — the Katy page covers how that works.

When the whole street breaks at once

A quirk of new subdivisions worth knowing: every house on the street got the same equipment, installed by the same crew, in the same few months. They tend to reach the same failure points at the same time.

That is useful information. If two neighbors have had the same part fail recently, mention it when you call. It points a technician straight at a likely cause and occasionally reveals a batch fault the manufacturer will cover.

Getting someone to come out

Fresno sits at the southern edge of the metro. Some Houston companies treat it as an outer zone with a trip charge, and some do not travel this far. Ask about it when you book rather than reading it on the invoice afterward.

It also affects emergency response time on a July afternoon, which is worth knowing before you need it rather than during.

Getting maintenance right from the start

On a system that is still young, maintenance genuinely pays. Change the filter monthly in summer, rinse the outdoor coil twice a year with a garden hose, and flush the condensate drain with distilled vinegar at the start and end of summer. Those three things prevent most of what brings a young system to an unscheduled stop.

Before you call

  • How old is the house, and is it still in builder warranty?
  • Has this room ever cooled properly, or did it change?
  • Have neighbors had the same problem?
  • Check the filter, both breakers, and the drain pan.
