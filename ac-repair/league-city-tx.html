In League City, the outdoor unit gets its own windstorm certificate

League City is in Galveston County, one of the fourteen coastal counties where the Texas Department of Insurance runs its windstorm inspection program. Most people know that program as a roofing rule. It also covers air conditioners. TDI lists air conditioner units among the items that need certification, and its own guidance for homeowners explains that when the AC is installed separately, TDI inspects it and issues a separate WPI-8 for the unit.

What the inspector is looking at is simple: whether the outdoor unit is anchored to its pad well enough to stay put in hurricane winds. An unanchored condenser can slide, tip, tear its refrigerant lines or become debris. If your home carries, or may someday need, wind coverage through the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association, the replacement should be anchored to the windstorm standard and inspected. Ask your installer before the job, not after.

Replacing an AC in League City? Ask three things: Will it be anchored to the windstorm standard? Will you schedule the TDI or engineer inspection? Will I get the WPI-8 for the unit?

Salt air and the coil

League City sits along Clear Creek and Clear Lake, a few miles from Galveston Bay. Salt carried inland settles on outdoor coils and corrodes the aluminum fins and copper tubing. A coastal coil can fail years sooner than the same unit inland. What helps:

  • Rinse the outdoor coil with fresh water, gently, a few times a year.
  • Ask about factory-coated or corrosion-resistant coils on a replacement.
  • Keep sprinklers from spraying the unit, which adds minerals to the salt.
  • Have the coil checked at every tune-up; small leaks start at corroded joints.

After the storm

Hurricane Ike in 2008 pushed floodwater and debris across the county, and Hurricane Beryl's winds in July 2024 knocked out power for days. After a storm, check that the outdoor unit is still level and on its pad, the refrigerant lines are intact and nothing has blown into the fan, before turning cooling back on. If the unit was underwater, do not run it; have it inspected first. In low-lying areas, a replacement is a good time to raise the unit on a platform.

Licensed work, and newer refrigerants

Every AC contractor in Texas must hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license from TDLR; confirm it on the TDLR license search. New systems made since 2025 use newer refrigerants such as R-454B and R-32, which changes pricing and parts. The free refrigerant change guide explains it. See also emergency repair and tune-ups.

Common questions

Does a new AC in League City need a windstorm inspection?

If you want the home to stay eligible for TWIA coverage, yes. Galveston County is a designated coastal area, and TDI lists air conditioner units among the items it certifies. It issues a separate WPI-8 for an AC unit installed and inspected on its own.

What does the windstorm inspector check on an AC unit?

Mainly that the outdoor unit is properly anchored to its pad so it resists hurricane winds.

Why do outdoor units fail sooner in League City?

Salt air corrodes the coil fins and tubing. Fresh-water rinsing, coated coils and regular checks extend their life.

Can I run my AC after it was flooded?

No. Have it inspected first. Water in the electrical components or compressor can cause damage or a hazard.
