A new Fulshear house, a young AC, and the paperwork that protects it

Fulshear has grown from a small town on FM 359 into one of the fastest-growing places around Houston, with Cross Creek Ranch, Fulbrook on Fulshear Creek, Jordan Ranch, Tamarron and more going up around it. That means most air conditioners here are young. When a young system fails, the most valuable call is often not to a repair company but to the paperwork:

  1. The builder's warranty. Early problems such as a disconnected duct, a poorly sloped drain line or a refrigerant leak at a joint are usually workmanship, and the builder's warranty is where to start.
  2. The equipment registration. Many manufacturers offer a longer parts warranty only if the unit was registered online within a set period after installation, often 60 days. Builders and buyers both forget. Find the model and serial numbers on the outdoor unit and check the manufacturer's site.
  3. The installer's labor warranty. Parts warranties rarely cover the labor to install them. Know who covers that and for how long.

Common first-summer problems

New houses have their own pattern of AC trouble, and most of it shows up in the first hot summer:

  • Refrigerant charge set in cool weather. If the system was charged during construction in winter, it may not be right for August. A technician can check it under real load.
  • Condensate drains that were not sloped or trapped correctly, or a float switch that trips the system off.
  • Upstairs rooms that stay warm because dampers were never balanced after move-in.
  • Dirty filters from construction dust still settling out of the house.

Built to the minimum

New homes are often built with equipment at the legal minimum efficiency, which in Texas has been 14.3 SEER2 for most split systems since 2023. That is fine, but it helps to know. When the first replacement comes due in ten or fifteen years, stepping up to a two-stage or variable-speed system is the chance to improve comfort and humidity control. Newer systems also use refrigerants such as R-454B; the free refrigerant change guide explains what changed.

Licensed help

Whoever works on your system should hold a Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license, which you can check on the TDLR license search. If your builder or warranty company sends its own contractor, you can still check the license. More: AC not cooling, leaks and ice and tune-ups.

Common questions

My Fulshear house is new and the AC stopped cooling. Who do I call first?

Check the builder's warranty and whether the equipment was registered for the manufacturer's extended parts warranty. Early failures are often workmanship the builder should cover.

How do I register my AC for the extended warranty?

Find the model and serial numbers on the outdoor unit's data plate and register on the manufacturer's website. Many brands require it within a set period, often 60 days after installation.

Why is my upstairs warm in a brand-new house?

Often the zone dampers or airflow were never balanced after move-in, or the system's charge was set in cool weather. Both are adjustable.

What efficiency is a builder-grade AC?

Usually the legal minimum, 14.3 SEER2 for most split systems in Texas since 2023.
