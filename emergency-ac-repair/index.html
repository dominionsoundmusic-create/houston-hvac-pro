What counts as an emergency

Not every breakdown needs an after-hours call at after-hours rates. In a Houston summer, these do.

  • Anyone vulnerable in the house. Infants, elderly residents, anyone with a heart or respiratory condition. Indoor temperatures climb fast here once the system stops, and heat illness is a genuine risk rather than a discomfort.
  • Burning smell, or a breaker that trips the moment you reset it. Both suggest an electrical fault. Leave the system off and call.
  • Water actively running from the indoor unit into a ceiling or wall.
  • Ice on the refrigerant lines with the system still running. Every hour it runs frozen risks the compressor.

If it is none of those and the house is merely warm, a next-morning appointment at standard rates will usually cost you meaningfully less than a midnight call-out for the same repair.

What to do in the first ten minutes

These are worth doing before anyone arrives, and occasionally they end the emergency.

  1. Turn the thermostat to Off. If the coil is frozen or the compressor is struggling, leaving it running makes the repair bigger.
  2. Check both breakers. The air handler and the condenser are usually separate. Reset once. If it trips again, stop.
  3. Look at the drain pan and the float switch. A full pan cutting power is one of the most common "total failure" calls, and it is a small job.
  4. Pull the filter out and look at it. If light will not pass through it, that is very likely your problem.
  5. Set the fan to On. With cooling off, this thaws a frozen coil over a few hours and moves air through the house meanwhile.

Keeping the house liveable while you wait

Houston heat indoors is a serious matter, not an inconvenience. While you wait:

  • Close blinds on the sun side — west-facing windows in the afternoon do more than most people expect.
  • Move to the lowest floor. Upstairs in a two-storey Houston house becomes dangerous hours before downstairs does.
  • Drink water before you feel thirsty, and check on anyone elderly in the house regularly rather than waiting for them to say something.
  • Do not use the oven. Cook nothing that heats the house.
  • Fans help only up to a point. Above about 95 degrees indoors, moving hot air over skin stops cooling you and starts doing the opposite.

If the indoor temperature is climbing past the mid-nineties and the repair is hours away, going somewhere air conditioned is the right call rather than a defeat.

What after-hours actually costs

Expect a call-out premium outside normal hours, and a larger one overnight, on Sundays and on holidays. That is reasonable — someone is getting out of bed.

What is not reasonable is a company that will not tell you the premium before dispatching. Ask for the after-hours rate and the diagnostic fee on the phone, before anyone drives out. Any company that will not say is telling you something.

Questions worth asking at 11pm

  • What is the after-hours call-out fee, and is the diagnostic separate?
  • Is the diagnostic credited if I approve the repair?
  • Do you carry common parts on the van, or will this be a second visit?
  • Are you licensed in Texas?
