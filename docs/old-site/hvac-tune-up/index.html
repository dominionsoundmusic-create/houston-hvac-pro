AC Maintenance and Tune-Ups in Houston

A Houston system runs two to three times the hours of one in a milder climate, so most maintenance advice you will read is written for a different machine. Here is what actually applies.

Why Houston is different

Most maintenance advice assumes a system that cools for four months. A Houston system runs hard from roughly March to November. That is two or three times the running hours, which means maintenance intervals written for the national average are wrong here.

The practical consequence: filters that a box says last three months last one in a Houston July, and a system skipped for two years has effectively skipped four or five years of wear elsewhere.

What you can do yourself

TaskHow often here
Change the filterMonthly in summer, every two months otherwise. Write the date on the edge in marker
Hose the outdoor coilTwice a year. Power off first, spray from the inside out, never with a pressure washer
Clear two feet around the condenserWhenever the shrubs need it. The unit has to breathe
Flush the condensate drainStart and end of summer. A cup of distilled vinegar down the access port
Check the pan and float switchWhenever you are up there. A full pan is a very common shutdown cause

Never use a pressure washer on a condenser coil. The fins are thin aluminium and they bend flat, which blocks the airflow the coil depends on. A garden hose is the right tool.

What a professional tune-up should actually cover

"Tune-up" covers everything from a genuine inspection to a ten-minute look and an upsell. A real one includes measurements:

  • Refrigerant pressures checked against the system's rated charge, and the temperature split across the coil.
  • Capacitor tested under load. A capacitor can read fine visually and fail under a summer start.
  • Amp draw on the compressor and blower, compared against the nameplate.
  • Electrical connections checked and tightened. Heat cycling loosens them over years.
  • Condensate drain cleared and the float switch tested, not just looked at.
  • Coils inspected and cleaned where needed.

Ask for the numbers afterwards. A tune-up that produces no readings produced no information.

When to schedule it

February or March, before the first genuinely hot week. Two reasons: the failures that maintenance catches tend to reveal themselves under load, and every HVAC company in Houston is booked solid from June onward. A tune-up in August is both harder to get and less useful.

Is a maintenance plan worth it

Depends entirely on what is in it. The ones worth having include two visits a year, priority scheduling in summer, and a discount on repairs. Priority scheduling is the part that matters most — in a Houston July the difference between today and Thursday is not a small thing.

The ones not worth having are a single annual visit at a small discount with no scheduling priority. That is a prepaid service call.

What maintenance genuinely prevents

It will not stop a compressor failing at year fifteen. What it reliably prevents is the cluster of small failures that cause most emergency calls: frozen coils from dirty filters, shutdowns from blocked drains, capacitor failures that could have been caught, and the slow efficiency loss from a coil nobody has cleaned in five years.

Put differently: it does not extend the life of the expensive part much. It substantially reduces the number of hot afternoons you spend waiting for someone.
