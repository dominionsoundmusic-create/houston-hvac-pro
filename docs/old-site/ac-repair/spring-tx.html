Is your Spring system still on R-22?

Spring grew up fast in the 1970s and 1980s, and plenty of houses in Champion Forest, Northgate Forest, Olde Oaks and the older Klein neighborhoods have had only one or two air conditioners since. If yours is more than about fifteen years old, look at the data plate on the outdoor unit. If it says R-22, you are running on a refrigerant the EPA stopped allowing to be produced or imported on January 1, 2020.

That does not mean the system is illegal or has to come out. It means every pound of R-22 now comes from recovered and recycled stock, and it is priced accordingly. A system that needs a small repair can still be worth fixing. A system that is leaking R-22 is a different conversation: topping it up every summer gets more expensive each year, and the leak does not stop. A good technician will find the leak and tell you plainly whether repairing it or replacing the system makes more sense.

Newer systems are changing too. Equipment made from 2025 onward uses newer refrigerants such as R-454B and R-32 rather than R-410A. If replacement is on the table, the free refrigerant change guide explains what that means before you get quotes.

The ductwork in a Spring attic

A forty-year-old system usually sits on forty-year-old ductwork, or on flexible duct that was swapped in at some point and has sagged since. In a Spring attic that reaches well above 130 degrees in August, a leaking or crushed duct is dumping cooled air into the hottest space in the house. The symptoms look like an AC problem: rooms at the end of long runs that never cool, a system that runs all afternoon, humidity that never quite comes down. The fix is often in the attic rather than the equipment. Ask whoever comes out to look at the ducts before quoting a new system.

No city permit, but the license still applies

Spring is unincorporated Harris and Montgomery County, so there is no city mechanical permit and no city inspector on an AC replacement. The state license is the protection that remains. Texas requires anyone who contracts to install or repair air conditioning to hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, and technicians working under them must be registered or certified. You can check any company's license on the TDLR license search in about a minute.

What usually goes wrong in a Spring summer

  • Capacitors and contactors. The small electrical parts that start the compressor and fan fail most often in the heat. Quick to replace, and the most common repair.
  • Frozen coils. A clogged filter or low refrigerant ices the indoor coil, and the system blows warm air until it thaws.
  • Blocked condensate drains. Spring's humidity pulls gallons of water out of the air each day, and algae in the drain line backs it up into the pan or the ceiling.
  • Tired compressors. On an old R-22 system, a failed compressor is usually where repair stops making sense.

Common questions

Can I still get R-22 for my AC in Spring?

Yes, but only from recovered and recycled stock, because production and import ended January 1, 2020. It is expensive, so a system that keeps leaking R-22 is usually better replaced than refilled.

Do I need a permit to replace my AC in Spring, TX?

Spring is unincorporated, so there is no city mechanical permit. The contractor must still hold a Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license from TDLR.

How do I check an AC company's license?

Search the company or license number on the TDLR license search. It shows whether the license is active and its class.

Why is one room in my Spring house always hot?

Often the ductwork, not the equipment: a crushed, disconnected or leaking duct in the attic, or a long run with too little airflow. Have the ducts checked before buying a new system.
