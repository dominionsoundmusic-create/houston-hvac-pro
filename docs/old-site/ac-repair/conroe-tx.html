In Conroe, the city wants to know who is doing the work

The City of Conroe is stricter than most of its neighbors about who touches your air conditioner. Its contractor registration rules say anyone seeking a mechanical permit must be registered with the city, and that every applicant must be licensed with the state and registered with Conroe before a permit can be issued.

That gives you two easy checks on anyone quoting a replacement inside the city: an active Texas Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license, which you can look up on the TDLR license search, and registration with the City of Conroe. A company that is missing either cannot legally pull the permit. Outside city limits, in unincorporated Montgomery County, there is no city permit, but the state license still applies.

Conroe is Entergy country

Most of the Houston area buys electricity through CenterPoint's wires and picks a retail provider. Conroe and much of Montgomery County are different: they are served by Entergy Texas, which runs its own residential efficiency programs, including an A/C tune-up program and programs for higher-efficiency HVAC equipment. Terms and amounts change, so ask the company doing your work whether they participate and whether your tune-up or new system qualifies before the work is done, not after.

Lake Conroe houses that sit empty

A lot of homes around Lake Conroe are weekend places or second homes, closed up for weeks at a time. Turning the air conditioner off to save money in a Texas summer is how those houses get mold. The AC is also the dehumidifier; with it off, humidity climbs, and damp gets into drywall, closets and furniture within days.

  • Leave cooling on at a higher setting, around 80, rather than off.
  • Consider a smart thermostat that shows indoor temperature and humidity from your phone and alerts you if the system stops.
  • Clear the condensate drain before leaving for a long stretch. A drain that clogs while nobody is home can spill for days.
  • Ask about a whole-home dehumidifier if the house stays damp even with the AC running.

What fails in a Conroe summer

Pine pollen and needles coat outdoor coils, so condensers here need their fins cleaned more often than in open suburbs. Capacitors and contactors fail in the heat, filters clog, coils freeze, and drain lines back up. If an older system is on R-22, a leak is usually the point to think about replacement; the free refrigerant change guide explains the newer refrigerants. See also tune-ups and repair cost.

Common questions

Does an AC contractor need to register with the City of Conroe?

Yes, to pull a mechanical permit inside the city. Conroe requires applicants to be licensed with the state and registered with the city before a permit can be issued.

Who is the electric utility in Conroe?

Entergy Texas serves Conroe and much of Montgomery County. It runs its own residential efficiency programs, including an A/C tune-up program. Ask your contractor about eligibility before the work.

Should I turn my AC off in an empty lake house?

No. Set it higher, around 80, so it keeps removing humidity. Turning it off in summer invites mold.

Why does my outdoor unit need cleaning so often?

Pine pollen, needles and leaves clog the coil fins, which cuts the unit's ability to shed heat and makes it run longer.
