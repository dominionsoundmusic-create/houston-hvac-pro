Emergency AC Repair: What to Do While You Wait in Houston

It happens at the worst possible time — the middle of a sweltering Houston summer, temperatures pushing past 95°F, and your air conditioner suddenly stops working. Your first call is to a trusted HVAC technician, but what do you do in the meantime? Whether you're waiting an hour or a few hours for emergency AC repair, there are smart, practical steps you can take to keep your home cooler, protect your family, and even help your technician diagnose the problem faster. This guide walks you through exactly what to do while you wait for help to arrive.

1. Check the Basics Before You Call (or While You Wait)

Before assuming the worst, do a quick inspection of the obvious culprits. Many emergency AC calls turn out to be simple fixes that homeowners can identify — and sometimes resolve — on their own.

  • Check your thermostat: Make sure it's set to "Cool" and the temperature is set lower than the current room temperature. Batteries die, settings get accidentally changed, and kids (or pets) bump thermostats more often than you'd think.
  • Inspect your circuit breaker: Head to your electrical panel and look for a tripped breaker. AC units draw a lot of power and can trip breakers, especially during peak summer heat. If you find a tripped breaker, reset it once — but if it trips again immediately, leave it alone and let the technician handle it.
  • Check the air filter: A severely clogged air filter can cause your AC to freeze up or shut down entirely. Locate your filter (usually near the return air vent or inside the air handler) and check its condition. If it's visibly clogged with dust and debris, go ahead and replace it.
  • Inspect the outdoor unit: Walk outside and look at your condenser unit. Make sure it's running (you should hear the fan and compressor). Check that nothing is blocking airflow — overgrown shrubs, lawn furniture, or debris can restrict performance.

Passing along what you found to your HVAC technician when they arrive saves valuable diagnostic time and gets your cool air back faster.

2. Turn Off Your AC System to Prevent Further Damage

If your AC is making strange noises — grinding, screeching, banging, or rattling — or if you notice ice forming on the refrigerant lines or the indoor unit, turn the system off at your thermostat right away. Running a damaged air conditioner can turn a manageable repair into a costly replacement.

Ice on your AC unit is a common sign of restricted airflow or low refrigerant. When you see this, switch the thermostat from "Cool" to "Fan Only." This allows the fan to keep running and helps melt the ice without risking further compressor damage. Give it 1–2 hours to fully thaw before your technician arrives — they'll thank you for it.

Continuing to run an AC that's clearly struggling is one of the most expensive mistakes Houston homeowners make during an emergency. When in doubt, shut it down and wait.

3. Keep Your Home as Cool as Possible

Houston heat is no joke, and even a few hours without AC can make your home dangerously uncomfortable — especially for young children, elderly family members, or pets. Here's how to manage the heat while you wait for your emergency AC repair:

  • Close blinds and curtains: Direct sunlight pouring through windows can raise your indoor temperature significantly. Close all blinds, curtains, or shades — especially on south- and west-facing windows.
  • Use ceiling fans strategically: Ceiling fans don't actually cool the air, but they create a wind-chill effect that makes people feel cooler. Make sure your fans are spinning counterclockwise in summer to push cool air downward.
  • Head to lower floors: Heat rises. If you have a two-story home, gather the family downstairs where it will be noticeably cooler.
  • Minimize heat sources inside: Avoid using the oven, running the dishwasher, or doing laundry. All of these appliances generate heat. Stick to cold meals or grab takeout if the wait is long.
  • Open windows strategically at night: If the outside temperature drops below your indoor temperature (usually after sundown), open windows on opposite sides of the house to create a cross-breeze.
  • Use portable or window fans: If you have them, deploy portable fans in occupied rooms to improve air circulation.

4. Check on Vulnerable Family Members and Pets

In Houston's extreme summer heat, indoor temperatures without air conditioning can climb dangerously fast. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real risks, particularly for infants, elderly adults, and anyone with heart or respiratory conditions.

Watch for signs of heat-related illness, including heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, or confusion. If anyone in your household shows these symptoms, move them to a cooler environment immediately — a neighbor's home, a local library, a shopping mall, or a cooling center.

Don't forget your pets. Dogs and cats overheat quickly and can't cool themselves the way humans do. Make sure they have access to cool water and a shaded, ventilated area. If your home is getting dangerously hot, don't hesitate to take your pets — and your family — somewhere cooler while you wait.

5. Document the Problem for Your Technician

While you're waiting for your emergency AC repair technician to arrive, take a few minutes to document what's happening with your system. This information helps your HVAC professional diagnose the issue faster and may even save you money on the service call.

Note the following and have it ready:

  • When did the AC stop working or start behaving strangely?
  • What sounds, if any, is it making?
  • Is the outdoor unit running? Is the indoor air handler running?
  • Did you notice any unusual smells — burning, musty, or chemical odors?
  • Have you had any recent maintenance or repairs done?
  • How old is your system, and do you have your warranty information?

If it's safe to do so, take a quick photo of your thermostat settings, your air filter, and your outdoor unit. This visual documentation can be surprisingly helpful for your technician.

6. Know When It's a True Emergency

Not every AC issue is a full-blown emergency, but in Houston, some situations absolutely require immediate professional attention. Call for emergency AC repair right away if:

  • Your indoor temperature is rising rapidly and reaching dangerous levels (above 90°F)
  • You smell burning or electrical odors coming from your unit
  • You see sparks, scorch marks, or smoke near your air handler or outdoor unit
  • You have elderly residents, infants, or medically vulnerable family members in the home
  • Your home is consistently above 80°F and not cooling down at all
