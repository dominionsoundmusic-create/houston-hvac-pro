The Woodlands: a planned town built inside a pine forest

George Mitchell began buying timberland in Montgomery County in 1964, and The Woodlands held its grand opening on October 19, 1974. From the start, the plan was to keep a large share of the native pine forest standing between the houses, roads and shopping. Drive through any village today and you see the result: homes tucked behind tall pines, wooded greenbelts, and yards that get more shade than sun.

The Woodlands is also not a city. It is run by The Woodlands Township, and it spreads across two counties: most villages are in Montgomery County, while Creekside Park is in Harris County. That split shows up in practical ways, from which company delivers your electricity to which county office handles anything that needs a county permit.

Trees and your AC: what helps and what hurts

The forest is the reason many people chose The Woodlands. It changes how a cooling system lives, in both directions.

Shade over the house

Pines that shade the roof and west walls cut the heat the house soaks up in the afternoon, so the system has less to remove. That is a real benefit worth keeping.

Needles in the coil

The outdoor unit sheds heat through thin fins. Needles, leaves and pollen packed against them act like a blanket, and the compressor runs hotter and longer for the same cooling.

Clear space around the unit

With the system switched off at the thermostat, rake debris away from the cabinet and keep shrubs trimmed back so air can move freely in and out.

Do not take it apart

Removing the top, spraying inside the cabinet or straightening fins near wiring is work for a technician. Tell the company you reach that the unit sits under pines.

Township design review comes before the new unit

This is the step newcomers miss. The Woodlands Township covenants require prior written approval from your village Residential Design Review Committee, or its staff, before anyone places, alters or repairs a structure or improvement on a lot with an existing home. Each developed village has its own committee of residents, and Covenant Administration staff review applications and issue the Township's own permits. Some neighborhoods have extra criteria on top of the Township standards.

A straight repair inside an existing system is not the same as moving a condenser, adding a second unit or installing a standby generator. If a replacement changes where equipment sits or how it looks from the street, ask Covenant Administration whether an application is needed before the work is scheduled. Township approval and any county permit are separate, so one does not stand in for the other.

  • Ask the company whether it has worked through Woodlands design review before and who files the application.
  • Register first: the Township asks applicants to set up an account on its online Civic Access portal before applying for a permit, so do that early if you will be filing.
  • Keep the location of the new condenser the same when you can, and ask whether screening is expected in your neighborhood.
  • Get it in writing on the quote: equipment, any permits, and who is responsible for approvals.

Two electric companies, one community

The Township says The Woodlands is served by two electric utilities, Entergy Texas and CenterPoint Energy Houston Electric, and which one you have depends on where you live. During Hurricane Beryl in July 2024, Community Impact reported that Entergy served the villages in Montgomery County, while CenterPoint served Creekside Park in Harris County. The difference matters. The Township notes that Entergy Texas is a regulated utility, so its customers do not pick a retail provider. In CenterPoint's area, you choose a retail provider and CenterPoint runs the wires.

For your air conditioner, the practical rule is the same either way. If the whole house is dark, report the outage to the utility that delivers your power. When the lamps come on yet the condenser stays quiet, the fault lives inside your own equipment or its circuit, and a utility crew cannot help with it. Phone an HVAC business instead.

Heating in The Woodlands when the first cold front arrives

Heating repair in The Woodlands usually starts with the first chilly October or November morning, when a system that sat idle all summer is asked to work. Before picking up the phone, make sure the thermostat reads Heat with a target warmer than the room, swap weak batteries, and look at the filter. A breaker that has tripped gets one reset; a second trip means you stop there and phone for help.

Gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm: the Township notes CenterPoint provides natural gas service in portions of The Woodlands. If you smell gas, get everyone outside first, then call your gas company's emergency line from outdoors (CenterPoint lists 888-876-5786 for gas leaks in the Houston area) or 911. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911.
