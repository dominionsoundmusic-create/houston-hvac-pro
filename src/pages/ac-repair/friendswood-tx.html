Friendswood: a Quaker colony that became a bedroom suburb

Two Quakers, Frank Brown and T. Hadley Lewis, settled the land in 1895 and named it in honor of the Society of Friends. Galveston County's museum calls it the only permanent Texas town that began as a Quaker colony. Farming, fig orchards in particular, carried the local economy for decades, and through the 1940s fewer than 500 people lived here.

Two events turned it into a suburb. Friendswood incorporated in 1960, and NASA's Manned Spacecraft Center, now Johnson Space Center, opened a few miles to the east in the early 1960s. NASA families moved in, farmland became subdivisions, and growth kept going through the 1970s and 1980s. The city sits mostly in Galveston County, with a part in Harris County. That history means many homes here are well past their first air conditioner.

Older homes, added rooms and tired ductwork

A house built in the 1960s or 1970s has usually changed shape. A garage turned into a game room, a sunroom off the back, a bigger primary suite: each change adds space the original system was never sized for. The added room is often the one that never quite cools, because it hangs off the end of a long duct run.

A bigger outdoor unit rarely fixes that. What a careful technician checks first is the airflow: whether the room has its own return path, whether the duct feeding it is the right size, and whether old flexible duct in the attic has sagged, torn or come loose at a joint. A separate ductless unit for the added space is another option worth pricing. In a home this age, ask whether the ducts are original before agreeing to replace equipment.

Clear Creek, Hurricane Harvey and flooded systems

In late August 2017, Hurricane Harvey's rain sent Clear Creek and its tributaries over their banks across Friendswood. Roughly 1,000 residents were rescued by boat, and city figures reported a few months later counted roughly 2,400 damaged homes. Since then the city and Galveston County have used federal disaster recovery grants to buy out flood-prone property, including a townhome complex on Frenchmans Creek.

Flooding touches more of an HVAC system than the outdoor unit. Water can reach an air handler in a garage or closet, ducts in low spots and the wiring that links it all together. If your home took on water, keep the system off until it has been inspected, and ask the technician to look at every part that was submerged. If you are replacing equipment in a flood-prone spot anyway, ask whether a raised platform for the condenser fits your lot.

Power out or AC out? Sorting it out in Friendswood

Friendswood is in TNMP's Gulf Coast territory, not CenterPoint's, so the outage steps differ from much of the Houston area.

The whole house is dark

That is a utility problem. Report it to TNMP online with its Report Outage button or at 888-866-7456, option 1. Your retail electric provider does not fix lines.

Lights on, AC silent

Look at the panel. If the AC breaker has tripped, reset it one time. TNMP warns that reporting an outage that turns out to be inside the house can bring a fee.

Breaker trips again

Stop there and leave it off. Tripping twice usually means something in the equipment is shorting or overheating, a job for a technician, not a homeowner.

Gas heat, safety and the first cold front

Some Friendswood homes run a gas furnace tucked into an attic or closet, others with a heat pump or electric strips. If the house ever smells like gas, get everyone out on foot, leave switches alone, then phone 911 and the gas utility shown on your bill once you are well away from the house. If a carbon monoxide detector goes off, empty the house and call 911. Do not reopen the furnace or relight anything yourself; that belongs to a trained technician.

Safe checks before calling are short: thermostat set to Heat and above room temperature, fresh batteries if it has them, a clean filter, and a quick look at the breaker. If the furnace still will not start, read furnace repair and call.

Permits for a new system in Friendswood

The City of Friendswood publishes a list of every permit it issues each month. Residential system replacements, such as removing and replacing a furnace, coil and condenser, appear there as mechanical permits filed by the contractor. Before you sign, make sure the quote names who applies for the permit and that the city inspection is included.

  • License number. Ask for the contractor's Texas ACR license and confirm it with TDLR.
  • Ducts in the scope. In a 1970s house, find out whether the price covers duct repairs or only equipment.
  • Matched parts. Ask whether the indoor coil is being replaced with the outdoor unit.
  • Flood history. Tell the contractor if the home or the equipment has ever been in water.
