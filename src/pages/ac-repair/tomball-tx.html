Tomball is an incorporated city in northwest Harris County with its own city government, set along State Highway 249, which locals call Tomball Parkway. Inside city limits or outside: what changes

Not sure which side you are on? Your property tax statement lists every taxing unit for your home, and the City of Tomball appears there only if you are inside its limits.

Inside the City of Tomball

The city has its own government and rules for building work. Before a replacement, ask the HVAC company whether the job needs a City of Tomball permit and inspection, and who will pull it.

Unincorporated, Tomball address

There is no city building department for your home. The state license requirement still applies, and a subdivision with deed restrictions may want to approve new outdoor equipment.

Gas, propane or all electric

How your Tomball home heats decides who you call when it does not. Check your bills and the equipment label so you can tell the assistant and the company right away.

New equipment, old ducts

A common Tomball story: an older house gets a new outdoor unit and the back bedroom is still warm. Ducts that were run decades ago may leak at the joints, sag in the attic or feed air back through a return that is too small for today's equipment. A correctly sized system cannot fix air that never reaches the room. When you collect replacement quotes, ask each company:

  • Did you measure the house? A load calculation looks at the actual home, not the old unit's size.
  • What about the ducts and the return? Ask whether they were inspected and what the quote does about them.
  • Are the indoor and outdoor parts matched? A new outdoor unit paired with an old indoor coil can cause repeat problems.
  • Is there a trip charge? For a home on acreage outside town, ask before the visit.
  • May I have your ACR license number? Run it through the TDLR search before anyone starts.

Before you call: quick checks

Make sure the thermostat is on the right mode with a sensible setting, swap a filter that is gray with dust, and give a tripped breaker a single reset. On a wooded or rural lot, look for leaves, grass clippings or vines packed against the outdoor unit, and clear them by hand with the system off. If the breaker trips again or the unit hums without starting, stop and call. Long outages after storms such as Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 are also a good reason to learn where your breaker panel is before you need it.
