That leaves you with the same Gulf Coast heat as everyone else in the Houston area, but a slightly different set of questions about permits, service areas and who to call. This page sorts them out, and explains how the free line connects you with a local contractor.

Fresno: unincorporated Fort Bend County

Fresno is a census designated place, a name the Census Bureau gives to a settled community that has no municipal government. It lies in eastern Fort Bend County, southwest of downtown Houston. The 2020 Census counted 24,486 people on about 8.5 square miles of land, up from 19,069 in 2010, more people than Richmond, the county seat, had in the same count.

For a homeowner, the practical point is simple: when a contractor asks what the local rules are, there is no city permit counter in Fresno to call. Ask the contractor to explain which rules apply at your address, and get it on paper. Keep your deed restrictions or homeowners association rules handy as well, since they may say where an outdoor unit or a backup generator can go.

From a ten person stop to a suburb of 24,000

The land under Fresno was patented in 1824 and was once surrounded by cotton plantations. The name reportedly came from a settler who had lived in Fresno, California. A post office opened in 1910, but the place stayed tiny: the Texas State Historical Association records only ten residents and one business by 1933. Houston's growth reached it in the 1970s and 1980s. The count rose to 3,182 by 1990 and 6,603 by 2000, then nearly tripled by 2010.

That timeline matters for your equipment. Fresno likely has a small core of older houses, a layer of homes from the 1990s and 2000s that may be on their second or third air conditioner, and newer subdivisions where the original system is still running. A technician will want to know which of those describes your house.

Utilities and county services in Fresno

  • ElectricityCenterPoint Energy lists Fresno in its electric delivery area, so CenterPoint owns the lines and restores outages, and you choose the retail electric provider that bills you.
  • Natural gasNot every street has it. Check whether you get a gas bill and which company sends it, and keep that company's emergency number handy if you have a gas furnace or water heater.
  • Local governmentFort Bend County handles roads, drainage and development permits here, with commissioners court as the elected body.
  • PermitsThe county requires a development permit for a new structure, or an addition, of 400 square feet or more. For questions about other work, the county Engineering department has a permits group.

Five questions to ask before a Fresno service visit

What is the visit fee?

Ask whether there is a trip or diagnostic charge, how much it is, and whether it comes off the repair if you go ahead.

Do you cover my address?

Say your street or ZIP code, not just "Fresno." Coverage lines vary from contractor to contractor.

What is your Texas ACR license number?

Write it down and look it up on the state's TDLR license search before work starts.

Who handles any permit?

For a replacement, ask which agency, if any, permits and inspects the job at your address, and who applies.

And the fifth: can I see what is wrong and what it costs on paper before you start? A clear written quote protects you whatever the job.

Getting through a hot spell or an outage

Fresno shares the Gulf Coast climate: long, humid summers and the occasional hard freeze. When the power is out for the whole house, the problem belongs to the company that delivers your electricity, and your bill names it. When lights work but the AC does not, run the safe checks: thermostat on Cool and below room temperature, a fresh filter, the breaker reset one time, and debris cleared from around the outdoor unit. A breaker that trips again stays off until a technician has looked.

After a long outage, give the system a few minutes after power returns rather than flipping the thermostat on and off. If it does not start, or the outdoor fan runs while the house stays warm, that is the time to call. If you ever smell gas, leave the house before calling 911 and your gas company. If a carbon monoxide detector sounds, get out and call 911.

Drainage, ditches and where the outdoor unit sits

Fort Bend County's Fresno Plan, a set of priorities from the county commissioner for the area, lists flood protection near the top, with roadside ditches and Mustang Bayou both named. It describes a regional detention basin, paid for with a federal grant the county secured with help from the Texas General Land Office, to hold stormwater during heavy rain, and county road crews clearing and regrading roadside ditches. Voters also approved a 2019 county bond with money set aside for Mustang Bayou.

None of that keeps a heavy storm from leaving water in a low yard for a while. If water has ever stood around your condenser, tell the company you are put through to, and ask whether a taller pad or a raised stand makes sense the next time the unit is replaced. Never run a unit that sat in floodwater until a technician has checked it.

Newer homes and older homes

If your Fresno home is new enough to still be under a builder warranty, problems such as a duct that came loose or a drain line that backs up may be the builder's to fix. Read the warranty, report the issue in the way it asks, and keep copies. Write down the model and serial numbers from both the indoor and outdoor units, because any contractor or manufacturer will ask for them.

Older homes have their own pattern: an aging system, ducts in a hot attic, and sometimes an add-on room that never cooled well. In either case, ask the technician to explain what failed and why before you approve a repair.
