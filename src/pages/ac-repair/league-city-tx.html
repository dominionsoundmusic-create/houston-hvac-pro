League City: from Butler's Ranch to a NASA suburb

George Washington Butler brought his family from Louisiana in 1854 and started a cattle operation where Chigger Bayou meets Clear Creek. For years the settlement was known as Butler's Ranch or simply Clear Creek. According to a Texas House resolution honoring the Butler family, Butler persuaded Galveston financier J. C. League to buy land nearby and lay out streets, and the town took League's name.

It stayed a small farm and ranch town until NASA's human spaceflight center rose in nearby Clear Lake in the 1960s. Then the subdivisions came, and they kept coming. The Census Bureau counted roughly 114,000 residents in 2020 on about 51 square miles, and close to three out of four homes are owner occupied, a high share for the Houston area.

TNMP or CenterPoint: which one serves your League City street?

In most Houston suburbs, one utility delivers power to every house. League City is different. It sits on the dividing line between Texas-New Mexico Power, known as TNMP, and CenterPoint Energy, and the 77573 ZIP code is split between them. Depending on your address, either company owns the wires to your meter.

That matters in two practical ways. First, during an outage you report it to the delivery utility, so find its name and outage number on your bill now, before a storm. Second, if a utility efficiency program helps pay for a new system, the program depends on which utility serves you. Whichever it is, Texas retail choice lets you buy power from the retail provider you pick, and neither the utility nor the retailer services air conditioners. When lights and outlets work but the AC sits silent, the fault lies in the cooling equipment itself.

Galveston County's windstorm program reaches the AC unit

Galveston County is one of the fourteen first tier coastal counties that the Texas Department of Insurance treats as a designated catastrophe area. Most people think of that as a roofing rule. It covers more than the roof.

What is inspected

TDI's program materials list air conditioner units and other mechanical equipment among the items that need certification, along with roofs, windows and garage doors.

What the inspector wants

An outdoor unit fastened down so it stays put in hurricane winds. TDI's rules for equipment call for a pad large enough that the condenser rests fully on it with room for the anchors.

What you get

After a TDI inspector or an appointed engineer approves the work, TDI issues a WPI-8 certificate of compliance, which TWIA generally requires before it will write wind and hail coverage.

What to ask before a League City replacement

Because so much of League City was built in the last few decades, many homes are reaching the age when the original system gives out. A replacement here involves a few questions that an inland buyer might not think of.

  • Will the outdoor unit be anchored to the windstorm standard? Ask for it in writing if your home is on the Galveston County side.
  • Who arranges the windstorm inspection? Find out whether a TDI inspector or an appointed engineer will look at it, and when.
  • Will I get the certificate? Keep the WPI-8 with your insurance papers. TDI keeps a searchable record of issued certificates too.
  • Does the job need a city permit? Ask the company whether it will pull any City of League City permit and schedule the inspection.
  • Was the house measured for size? A load calculation, rather than copying the old unit's tonnage, catches changes like new windows or an enclosed patio.

A few checks while you wait

Look at the thermostat first: Cool mode, a setting below the current temperature, and working batteries. Pull the filter and hold it up to a light; if you cannot see through it, replace it. A tripped breaker for the AC may be reset one time, and if it trips again it stays off. Outside, rake grass clippings and leaves away from the condenser. If water is pooling near the indoor unit, our page on an AC leaking water explains the usual causes. Leave refrigerant, wiring and anything behind a panel to the technician.
