Katy: a railroad town that became a three county suburb

Settlers first called this stretch of coastal prairie Cane Island, after a creek full of cane, and by 1847 it was a stagecoach stop on the San Felipe Road. The town was platted in 1895, got its post office and its name in January 1896, and sat on the Missouri, Kansas and Texas rail line. Rice farming and rice mills carried it for decades. Katy did not incorporate until 1945.

Today the city counted 21,894 people in the 2020 census and reaches into Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties. Most people with a Katy address actually live outside it. Cinco Ranch, for example, is an unincorporated community of nearly 17,000 people about 10 miles southeast of the city. That matters for permits, for which county handles flood questions and for who you call when something goes wrong.

Katy homes and the Barker Reservoir

The Addicks and Barker reservoirs are federal flood control lakes that sit dry most of the time. During Hurricane Harvey in 2017 they filled, and some Katy area neighborhoods learned they were built inside the Barker flood pool.

What happened upstream

The Army Corps of Engineers closed the reservoir gates on August 25, 2017. Water backed up onto private land, and Fort Bend County leaders named Canyon Gate, Kelliwood, Cinco Ranch and areas along Mason Road among the neighborhoods that flooded.

What a court found

A federal claims judge later found the government responsible for flooding properties upstream of the reservoirs, and found the counties knew subdivisions were going up inside the Barker design flood pool without doing enough to warn buyers.

What it means for your AC

If your home flooded then or since, a condenser or air handler that sat in water deserves a careful look, even if it seemed to run fine afterward. If you are replacing equipment, ask whether raising the condenser and air handler makes sense for your lot.

Growth, new houses and first replacements

Katy ISD shows how fast the area keeps filling in. Demographers reported that the district gained about 14,870 students, or 18.6 percent, over five years, more than any other Houston area district. Most of that growth sits in the northwest part of the district, where master-planned communities such as Elyson and Cane Island are still being built.

Safe checks before you call

  • Thermostat. Set it to Cool, lower than the room is now, and put in fresh batteries when the display looks dim or blank.
  • Filter. A filter packed with dust can freeze the coil and stop the airflow. Swap it if you can see it is clogged.
  • Breaker. Reset a tripped AC breaker one time. A second trip means the circuit needs a professional.
  • Outdoor unit. Pull grass clippings, leaves and toys away from the condenser so it can breathe.
  • Water at the indoor unit. A full drain pan or a tripped float switch can shut the system down. Note it and tell the company.

Gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm? Gas service in the Katy area comes from either CenterPoint Energy or SiEnergy, depending on the address. If you smell gas, get everyone out, then call 911 and the gas company named on your bill from outside. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911.

Power, gas and permits for a Katy address

CenterPoint Energy owns the poles, wires and meters in the Katy area and handles outages. Katy is in the part of Texas with retail electric choice, so the company that bills you is the retail provider you picked, not CenterPoint. When the whole street goes dark, report it to CenterPoint. When only the AC is down, the problem is in the equipment or its circuit, which an HVAC company handles.

Inside the city limits, Katy's Permit and Building Services division handles permits and inspections. The city lists installing air conditioning and heating systems as work that needs a permit, works from the 2021 International codes, and has mechanical contractors register with the city every year. Outside the city, different rules can apply depending on the county, so ask the company to spell out which permit, if any, your replacement needs and to list it on the quote.

Questions to ask a Katy HVAC company

  • What is your Texas ACR license number? Then check it on the state's license search before work starts.
  • Is my house inside Katy city limits? It decides whether the city permit applies.
  • Did this unit or its wiring ever sit in floodwater? Ask them to look, especially near the reservoirs.
  • Did you size the new system with a load calculation? A replacement should fit the house as it is now, not just copy the old tonnage.
  • What refrigerant does the new system use? New equipment has moved to newer refrigerants, and the indoor and outdoor parts must match.
