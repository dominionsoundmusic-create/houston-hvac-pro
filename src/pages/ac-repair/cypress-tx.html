Cypress: a rural crossroads that became one of Houston's biggest suburbs

Cypress sits along US 290, the Northwest Freeway, roughly 24 miles northwest of downtown Houston, inside the Cypress Creek and Little Cypress Creek watershed. German families settled the area in the 1840s, and Sam Houston's army passed through in March 1836 on its way toward the fight at San Jacinto. For most of the next 150 years it stayed farm and ranch country.

That changed in the 1980s, when large subdivisions and shopping centers began filling in the land. The two ZIP codes most tied to the name, 77429 and 77433, counted about 200,800 residents between them in 2020, and building has continued since. Most homes here are a generation old or younger, which means fewer worn-out 1970s ducts and more questions about warranties, sizing and second floors.

The second floor problem in Cypress homes

Ask around any Cypress subdivision in August and you will hear the same complaint: the living room is cold and the kids' rooms are not. Here is what usually causes it and what tends to fix it.

Causes of a hot upstairs and the usual fixes
What is happeningWhat a technician may suggest
Bedrooms upstairs have supply vents but few or small returns, so stale warm air has nowhere to goAdding or enlarging return air paths so each floor can actually exchange air
Flexible ducts in the attic are crushed, kinked, disconnected or leaking into the hot atticRepairing, sealing or rerouting the runs that feed the upstairs rooms
One thermostat downstairs controls both floors and shuts the system off earlyZoning with dampers and a second thermostat
A large house is served by a single system that was sized tightA separate system for the second floor, decided by a proper load calculation

Start with the airflow questions before anyone prices a second unit. Many upstairs complaints turn out to be duct problems, which usually cost less to correct than new equipment.

When a young Cypress system fails early

A five year old air conditioner should not be giving up, and when it does, the paperwork matters as much as the repair. Before you approve anything, pull out your closing documents and look for two separate warranties: the builder's warranty, which can cover installation workmanship such as a poorly connected duct or drain line, and the equipment maker's warranty, which covers parts. Some equipment warranties only reach their full length if the unit was registered after installation, so it is worth asking the company to read the model and serial numbers and check.

A refrigerant leak at a joint, a drain line that was never pitched right, or ducts that were left pinched behind a wall in the first few years often trace back to how the system was put in rather than to bad luck. Ask the technician to note what failed and why on the invoice. That record helps if you take the question back to the builder.

Safe checks for a Cypress homeowner before you call

A few things are safe to look at yourself and can save a service call:

  • Thermostat. Make sure it is on Cool, set below the room temperature, and that the batteries are not dead. Two-story homes often have two thermostats, so check the right one.
  • Filter. Newer homes sometimes have filters in ceiling returns on both floors. A filter caked with dust can starve the system of air and freeze the coil.
  • Breaker. A tripped AC breaker gets one reset, no more. A second trip means something is wrong, so leave it alone and phone for help.
  • Outdoor unit. Clear grass clippings, leaves and anything stacked against the condenser so it can breathe.
  • Drain. Look for water around the indoor unit in the attic or closet. Many systems have a safety switch that shuts cooling off when the drain backs up.

Anything involving refrigerant, electrical parts inside the unit or gas belongs to a technician. A rotten egg smell means walk out of the house, then phone the gas utility's emergency number or 911 once you are outdoors. A sounding carbon monoxide alarm means the same: everybody outside, then 911. For more symptoms, read why an AC is not cooling.

No city hall: who handles what in Cypress

Cypress is an unincorporated community in Harris County and lies inside Houston's extraterritorial jurisdiction, but it is not part of any city. That surprises newcomers. There is no Cypress mayor, no city utility department and no city permit counter, so the usual advice to "check with the city" does not apply. When you replace a system, ask the HVAC company what permits, if any, apply at your exact address and whether it takes care of them, and get that answer on the written quote.

Electric service in Cypress runs over CenterPoint Energy's lines, and because the area is in the competitive Texas market, you pick the retail provider that sends your bill. CenterPoint handles outages and the wires. Neither company services cooling equipment. Lights on but no cold air points to the system itself, which is a job for an HVAC business.

Storms, outages and a creek named for the trees

Cypress is far enough inland that hurricanes arrive weaker, but not far enough to escape them. Hurricane Ike in 2008 knocked out power across the Houston area, and Harvey in 2017 sent Cypress Creek out of its banks. If your house lost power for days, turn the system off at the thermostat during the outage and wait until power has been steady before switching cooling back on. If the outdoor fan will not spin or the unit hums and stops when power returns, switch it off and phone for help instead of letting it struggle.

If water ever reached your outdoor unit, do not run it until a technician has looked at it. Flooded electrical parts and controls are a safety issue, not just a repair. When the time comes to replace a unit in a low spot near the creek, ask about setting the new one on a raised stand.

Questions to ask a Cypress HVAC company

  • What is your Texas ACR license number? Look it up on TDLR before work begins.
  • Is there a trip or diagnostic charge? Get the amount before anyone drives out.
  • Did you check the ducts upstairs? Especially if one floor is the complaint.
  • What is still under warranty? Ask them to check the serial number on a young system.
  • For a replacement, did you run a load calculation? A bigger unit is not automatically the fix for a hot second floor.
