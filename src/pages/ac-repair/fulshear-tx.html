Three warranties to check before you pay for a repair

The exact terms are in your closing papers and equipment booklets. These are the questions to answer first.

The builder warranty

Covers workmanship for a set time after closing. A duct that came loose, a drain line with no slope or a refrigerant joint that leaks from day one may fall here. Report it the way the warranty asks, usually in writing.

The equipment warranty

Covers parts from the manufacturer. Some makers offer a longer term only if the unit was registered online within a short window after installation. Find the model and serial numbers and check the maker's website.

The labor coverage

A parts warranty rarely pays for the hours to install the part. Find out whether the original installer covers labor, and for how long, before you agree to a bill.

Problems that show up in a new home's first summers

  • A drain line that backs up. If the condensate line was not sloped or trapped well, water collects and a safety float switch shuts the system off. Look for water in the pan under the indoor unit.
  • A warm upstairs. Dampers that were never adjusted after move-in, or ducts that were pinched during construction, can starve upstairs rooms of air.
  • A filter clogged fast. Fine construction dust keeps settling for months. Check the filter monthly the first year and change it when it looks gray.
  • A system that never shuts off. On the hottest afternoons long run times can be normal. A change from last summer, or rooms that will not get below the high 70s, is worth a service call.
  • A room that has never cooled. If it has been warm since the day you moved in, it is likely a design or installation issue, which points back to the builder warranty.

Safe checks before you call

Set the thermostat to Cool and a few degrees below the room temperature, and replace its batteries if the screen is blank. Pull the filter and swap it if it is dirty. If the outdoor unit is silent, check for a tripped breaker and reset it a single time; if it trips again, leave it alone. Clear away grass clippings, toys or bags that have blown against the outdoor unit. Anything involving refrigerant, wiring or the gas furnace is for a technician.

If you ever smell gas inside, get everyone out first and make the 911 and gas company calls from outside. If a carbon monoxide alarm goes off, leave the house and call 911.

Planning ahead for the first replacement

Builders usually choose equipment that meets the code minimum for efficiency. That is perfectly workable. When the first replacement comes due years from now, it is a chance to ask about two stage or variable speed equipment, which can run longer at low speed and pull more moisture out of the air in Fort Bend's sticky summers. Keep a folder with the model numbers, install date and every service invoice, because the next contractor will ask.
