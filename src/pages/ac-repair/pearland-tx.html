Pearland: from pear orchards to a city of new subdivisions

The town took its name from the pear trees that grew around an early railroad stop south of Houston. The Galveston hurricane of 1900 wiped out those orchards, and for decades Pearland stayed a small farm town. It incorporated in 1959. The city sits about 20 miles south of downtown Houston, mostly in Brazoria County, with smaller pieces in Harris and Fort Bend counties.

The real change came after 2000. Census counts show 37,640 residents in 2000, 91,252 in 2010 and 125,828 in 2020, and the city estimated about 132,900 by January 2024. In a 2016 release the Census Bureau ranked Pearland seventh among the nation's fastest growing cities of 50,000 or more for the year ending July 2015. A city that more than tripled in 20 years is full of homes whose first or second air conditioner is now reaching middle age.

A Pearland home by building era: what to ask about

A rough pattern only. Many homes have had equipment swapped already, so the technician who sees your system has the final word.

Before 2000

Homes from Pearland's quieter decades, before the big subdivisions. Systems here may be on their third round of equipment. Ask whether the ductwork is as old as the house and whether the refrigerant in the old unit is still easy to get.

2000 to 2010

The decade the population more than doubled. Original builder systems from this period are now 15 to 25 years old. A repair can still make sense, but ask for the age of the compressor and a written repair and replace comparison.

2010 and newer

Larger two-story homes sometimes have two systems, one per floor. Ask whether a warm upstairs is a sizing or duct issue rather than a failed part, and keep any manufacturer warranty paperwork handy when you call.

Clear Creek, Hurricane Harvey and the outdoor unit

In August 2017 Hurricane Harvey pushed Clear Creek to what local reporting described as a 500 year flood where it crosses State Highway 288 in Pearland. More than 1,000 Pearland homes and about 30 businesses flooded, and 205 residents were rescued by boat. Afterward the city began a master drainage plan with Brazoria County Drainage District No. 4.

For air conditioning, the lesson is about the outdoor unit and the electrical parts inside it. A condenser that has sat in water should stay off until a technician has checked it, because water in the controls and wiring is a shock and fire risk. If your home is in a low spot near the creek and the system is due for replacement anyway, ask the company whether setting the new condenser on a raised stand is practical for your lot and allowed by the city.

CenterPoint, TNMP and who to call when the power is the problem

Pearland is in the competitive electric market, so you pick a retail provider for your bill while a wires company delivers the power. CenterPoint Energy is the main delivery company in Pearland. TNMP, Texas-New Mexico Power, serves a small portion of the town, so two neighbors a few streets apart can have different outage lines. Look at your bill to see which one is yours.

Before you report an outage to TNMP, the company asks customers to check their own breakers first: if the problem turns out to be inside the house, a fee may follow. That is good advice on either system. When lights and outlets work but the AC is silent, the safe step is a single reset of its breaker. A breaker that snaps off a second time is telling you to stop and get a technician.

CenterPoint Energy is also the natural gas company in Pearland. If you ever smell gas, leave the house on foot with everyone, do not flip switches or start a car, and call 911 and CenterPoint from a safe distance. Treat a beeping carbon monoxide detector just as seriously: clear the house, then dial 911.

Replacing a system in Pearland: the permit and the paperwork

The City of Pearland requires permits for work on a building's mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems. Contractors must register with the city before they can apply, which means holding the right State of Texas license and filing proof of insurance, and inspections are scheduled through the city's online permit system. Pearland's permit office is at 3523 Liberty Drive.

  • Who handles the permit? The quote should say the contractor applies for it and books the city inspection.
  • Is the company registered with Pearland? A company that has never worked in the city may not have its account set up yet.
  • What is the ACR license number? Check it on the state's TDLR license search before you sign.
  • Was a load calculation done? A 2000s Pearland home with new windows or extra attic insulation may need a different size than the builder installed.
  • Does the price include the indoor coil? Replacing the outdoor unit alone on an old coil can cause repeat trouble with newer refrigerants.
