Cities, townships and unincorporated areas

Greater Houston mixes three kinds of places, and the difference matters when equipment is replaced. Incorporated cities such as Houston, Sugar Land, Pearland, Katy and League City run their own permit offices. Kingwood is part of the City of Houston, so Houston's rules apply there. The Woodlands is governed by a township with its own residential design review. Cypress, Spring and Fresno have no city government at all.

You do not need to sort this out before you call. It helps to know it when you compare quotes for a replacement: ask each company whether a permit or design approval applies at your address, who obtains it, and whether the inspection is included.

Who owns the wires where you live

Power outages are common in Houston hurricane season, and a dead air conditioner during an outage is a utility problem, not an HVAC one. Across most of the area, CenterPoint Energy delivers electricity and you buy it from the retail provider you choose. Parts of Montgomery County, including Conroe and some neighborhoods of The Woodlands (which is split between two utilities), are on Entergy Texas lines, and much of Galveston County, including Friendswood and League City, is on TNMP lines. The company that delivers your power is printed on your bill, and that is who to call about an outage.
