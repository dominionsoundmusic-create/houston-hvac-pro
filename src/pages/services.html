Cooling services

In a city where summer afternoons sit in the mid 90s for weeks, cooling problems are the most urgent ones. Cooling work splits into four kinds of visit.

AC repair

A system that blows warm, short cycles (turns on and off every few minutes), freezes up or will not start. A careful technician tests before replacing anything: electrical readings on the capacitor and contactor, refrigerant pressures, airflow and the condensate drain. See AC repair in Houston.

Air conditioning repair, part by part

If you want to understand the parts that wear out in Gulf Coast humidity, from coils to blower motors, and how each repair is done, the air conditioning repair page covers them one at a time.

Emergency and after-hours AC repair

For a house that is heating up overnight, an older relative or a baby at home, or a system that quit after a storm knocked out power. The line answers at any hour; what the company charges for a night or weekend visit is up to the company, so ask. See emergency AC repair.

AC installation and replacement

A new system should be sized by a load calculation of your house, not by the size of the old unit. A complete quote names the equipment model numbers, the efficiency rating, what happens to the ductwork and who pulls the permit. Inside Houston city limits, the Houston Permitting Center requires a mechanical permit for installing or replacing a condensing unit, followed by a city inspection. See AC installation in Houston.

Heating services

Houston homes heat in two main ways: a gas furnace, usually in the attic or a closet, or electric heat, either a heat pump or resistance heat strips in the air handler. They fail differently, so they have separate pages.

Furnace repair

A gas furnace that clicks but will not light, lights and shuts off, or fills the house with a burning smell on its first run of the season. Burners, gas valves and flue venting are technician work only. See furnace repair in Houston.

Heating repair: heat pumps and electric heat

A heat pump that blows cool air in heat mode, runs on emergency or auxiliary heat all night, or ices over and never defrosts. See heating repair.

Smell gas? Leave the house without flipping switches, then call 911 and the gas utility's leak line from a safe distance. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911.

Maintenance and commercial systems

HVAC tune-up and maintenance

A spring cooling check and a fall heating check catch weak parts, dirty coils and slow drains before they turn into a breakdown. The tune-up page explains how often to book one, and is a tune-up worth it weighs the evidence.

Commercial HVAC

Rooftop package units, split systems in offices and shops, and the cooling a restaurant kitchen needs. Commercial work is a different scale, and Texas license classes reflect it. See commercial HVAC in Houston.

What the line does not take calls about

The same phone number also handles Houston roofing and pressure washing calls, which is why the assistant asks what you need first. On this site, though, the subject is heating and cooling only. These jobs belong to other trades:

  • Plumbing, including water heaters, drains and gas piping to appliances. The water heater guide explains who to call.
  • Electrical work beyond the HVAC equipment itself, such as panel upgrades or new circuits.
  • Duct cleaning sold as a separate service. Duct repair and sealing that is part of an HVAC job is a question for the HVAC company.
  • Roofing and pressure washing are not covered on this site.
  • Appliance repair: refrigerators, washers, dryers and ovens.

Before you approve any work

The companies you reach are independent businesses. You decide whether to hire them, and you are free to get a second quote. Ask for the diagnosis in plain words, the price in writing and the company's Texas license number.
