What this Houston HVAC line is, and what it is not

When the AC quits on a muggy Houston evening or the heat will not kick on during a cold snap, you need one number that answers, not a page of ads and a string of voicemails.

Houston Air & Heating is that number for HVAC help in Houston. It is a free phone line and this website. It is not a heating and air conditioning contractor: it has no technicians, no service vans and no license, and it never repairs, installs or prices anything. What it does is answer your call at any hour, ask a couple of quick questions, and connect you with an HVAC company in your part of town that is independent and locally owned.

The HVAC company is the one that looks at your system, explains what is wrong, prices the job, carries out the work and sends you the bill. You deal with it directly. The line never charges you, and the companies may pay it a referral fee, which this site always tells you up front.

Why Houston systems work so hard

Heat is only half of it. Houston air carries moisture in from the Gulf of Mexico for much of the year, so an air conditioner here has two jobs at once: lower the temperature and pull water out of the air. That is why a system that cools but leaves the house sticky usually has a problem worth checking, and why drain lines clog and drip pans overflow so often in this area.

Long, hot summers

Normal afternoon highs at Bush Intercontinental Airport sit in the mid 90s through July and August, based on the National Weather Service's 1991 to 2020 records. In 2025 the Weather Service office for Houston and Galveston counted 145 days that reached 90 degrees or more, beating the old record of 142 set in 2011. A system that runs nearly nonstop for months wears down the compressor, the fan motors and the electrical parts a little more every week, and a weak part tends to fail on the hottest afternoon of the week. A spring tune-up is a good chance to find that part before it fails.

Hurricane season and long outages

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30, and its peak falls around September 10. When Hurricane Beryl came ashore near Matagorda as a Category 1 storm early on July 8, 2024, CenterPoint Energy reported more than 2.2 million of its customers without power, and by July 19, eleven days of July heat later, it had restored more than 99 percent of them. After an outage, have the system looked at if it trips a breaker, hums without starting or blows warm air once the power returns, and read the emergency AC repair page for how to keep the house safer until help arrives.

Freezes do happen here

Houston heating systems usually get an easy winter, so a hard freeze tends to find their weak spots. In February 2021 an arctic outbreak dropped Bush Intercontinental to 13 degrees on February 16, and the Weather Service described road closures, power outages, loss of heat and burst pipes across the region. ERCOT, the grid operator for most of Texas, ordered rotating outages starting early on February 15 as power plants dropped offline in the cold. A heat pump or furnace that has sat idle all year deserves a fall check before the first real cold front. The furnace repair and heating repair pages cover what goes wrong.

Safe checks before you call

  • Thermostat: check that it says Cool or Heat and that the target is a few degrees beyond what the room reads now. A blank or faint screen usually means it needs batteries.
  • Air filter: a clogged filter starves the system of air and can lead to ice on the indoor coil. Put in a new one if it looks gray and packed.
  • Breaker: if the AC or furnace breaker has tripped, flip it back on once. A second trip points to a fault, so leave it off and phone for help.
  • Outdoor unit: pull leaves, grass clippings and debris away so air can move through the coil.

Gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm: do not touch switches or try to find the leak. Get everyone outside first. For a gas smell, call 911 and your gas utility's leak line once you are a safe distance away (CenterPoint Energy serves much of the area). For a carbon monoxide alarm, call 911 from outside. Refrigerant, wiring and anything inside a furnace are jobs for a licensed technician.

Four questions to ask the company you reach

The company is independent, so checking it is your job. These take a few minutes and protect you on any visit.

What is your Texas ACR license number?

Any company that contracts heating and air conditioning work in Texas needs an ACR contractor license from TDLR. A company that hesitates to give you the number is a company to pass on.

What does the visit cost?

Ask about the service call or diagnostic charge before anyone drives out, and whether night or weekend visits cost extra.

What part failed, and why?

A plain explanation and the failed part named lets you compare a second opinion and understand what you are paying for.

Can I see the price in writing first?

Get the repair or replacement price written down before work begins. For a new system, ask whether the company will pull the permit your city requires.
