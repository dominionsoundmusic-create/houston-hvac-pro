If you searched for water heater repair in Houston, TX, here is the honest answer up front: Houston Air & Heating cannot help with it. The line only handles heating and air conditioning calls, and in Texas a water heater falls under plumbing, a separate licensed trade. What this page can do is point you to the right kind of professional, show you how to check that person's license in a couple of minutes, and walk through the safety steps that matter before anyone arrives.

The same caution applies if an HVAC company offers to swap your water heater. Some businesses hold both a heating and cooling license and a plumbing license, and that is fine. The point is to know which license the job needs and to see it before work begins.

Water heater safety in a Houston home

Most water heater trouble is an inconvenience. These situations are not, and they come before any repair call.

Gas smell near a gas water heater

Walk out of the house right away and take everyone with you. CenterPoint Energy, which supplies natural gas across much of the Houston area, says not to use switches, phones or anything that could spark on the way out and not to touch the meter or valves. From a safe distance, call 911 and CenterPoint's gas leak line at 888-876-5786.

Carbon monoxide alarm sounding

A gas water heater burns fuel and vents exhaust, just like a furnace. If a CO alarm goes off, or several people feel headachy, dizzy or sick at once, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911. Do not go back in until responders say it is safe.

Hot water spraying or pouring out

Scalding water can burn in seconds. Keep children and pets back, stay clear of the relief valve outlet, and call a plumber. Do not plug or cap the relief pipe to stop the flow.

Water around an electric unit or outlet

Water and electricity together are dangerous. Stay out of standing water near an electric water heater or nearby outlets, and let a licensed professional deal with the wiring.

Why water heater repair in Houston is a plumber's job

Texas licenses plumbers through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners under the Plumbing License Law in Chapter 1301 of the Occupations Code. The law's definition of plumbing reaches the appliances and piping that supply, distribute or circulate water or gas in a building, which is exactly what a tank or tankless water heater is. The board's rules also say that installing a gas-fueled water heater or cutting into fuel gas piping is not the kind of incidental work that an unlicensed handyman may do.

Heating and air conditioning is a different license. TDLR issues Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor licenses for heating, cooling and refrigeration work, and state law goes the other way too: a plumbing license alone does not let someone do air conditioning contracting. So an ACR license is the right thing to ask for when the furnace or AC fails, and a plumbing license is the right thing to ask for when the water heater does.

What this means when you call around

  • Ask for a plumbing license number, not just a business name or a heating and cooling license.
  • Ask whether a master plumber is responsible for the company's work. Texas requires a plumbing company that serves the public to have a Responsible Master Plumber.
  • Ask who pulls the permit if the tank is being replaced.
  • Ask for a written price before work starts, and whether haul-away of the old tank is included.

How to check a Texas plumber's license

The plumbing board runs a free Public License Search on its website. It takes a couple of minutes and shows far more than whether a license exists.

  1. Get the license number. Ask the plumber or company for it. Master Plumber license numbers start with the letter M.
  2. Open the TSBPE Public License Search at tsbpe.texas.gov and choose the plumbing license option.
  3. Enter the number without the letter. The board's instructions say to type the digits only.
  4. Open the record and read it. It shows whether the license is current, when it expires, endorsements, any disciplinary actions and whether a certificate of insurance is on file.
  5. For a company, check its Responsible Master Plumber as well, and confirm that person's license and insurance certificate.
  6. Save a screenshot of the record with your paperwork.

The board also publishes a Find-a-Plumber map built from the same license data, which can help if you are starting from scratch.

Replacing a water heater in the City of Houston

According to the Houston Permitting Center, a plumbing permit is required to replace or install a water heater inside city limits. The permit has to be obtained by a master plumber who has registered a state license with the City of Houston. For a single-family home no plan review is needed, but the work must pass an inspection, and the permit stays valid for 180 days. Because the plumber pulls the permit, ask whether it is included in the price you are quoted.

Those rules cover the City of Houston itself. Suburban cities and unincorporated areas set their own requirements, so if you live outside Houston city limits, ask the plumber which rules apply and check with your city's building department.

A safer setting while you wait

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends keeping household hot water at 120 degrees Fahrenheit or lower to help prevent scald burns, especially with young children or older adults in the home. It suggests checking with a cooking thermometer under a faucet first thing in the morning rather than relying on the dial. If your water heater runs on gas and you are not sure how to adjust it, leave that to the plumber.
