AC Installation and Replacement in Houston

The brand on the box matters far less than the size of the system and the quality of the install. Here is what actually determines whether a new system performs, and what a proper quote has to show you.

Sizing matters more than brand

The most consequential decision in a new system is not which badge is on it. It is whether it is the right size, and the most common error is oversizing.

A system too large cools the house to temperature quickly and shuts off. That sounds efficient and is the opposite. Air conditioning removes humidity by running, and in Houston humidity is half the comfort problem. A short-cycling oversized unit leaves a house that is cold and clammy, and it wears the compressor out faster through repeated starts.

Proper sizing comes from a Manual J load calculation — square footage, insulation, window area and orientation, ceiling height, duct condition. If a contractor sizes your system by looking at the old one or by square footage alone, they are guessing. Ask to see the calculation.

The refrigerant question, if you are replacing now

Since January 2025, new residential systems use R-454B rather than R-410A, following the EPA's global warming potential cap under the AIM Act. A new system today is on the current platform, which is the right side of that transition to be on.

It also means a new system is not compatible with your old refrigerant, and the two cannot be mixed or converted. That is normal and expected — it simply means replacement is a whole-system decision rather than a part swap.

Installation quality beats efficiency rating

This is the part the industry underplays. A high-efficiency system installed badly will underperform a mid-range system installed well, and most of the difference is in things you cannot see from the driveway.

  • Duct sizing and sealing. Leaky attic ducts in Houston lose cooling into a space that is already 130 degrees.
  • Correct refrigerant charge. Weighed in, not guessed by pressure alone.
  • Airflow set to the manufacturer's specification rather than left at the factory default.
  • The condensate path, including a properly fitted float switch. This is what stops a future leak becoming ceiling damage.

Timing

If the choice is yours, replace in spring or autumn. Summer in Houston means the longest waits, the least scheduling flexibility and the least room to negotiate. A system limping through August that you already know is finished is better replaced in October than in next July's emergency.
