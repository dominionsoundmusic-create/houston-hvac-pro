Houston water is hard, and that is the whole story

Most water heater failures in this area trace back to one thing: dissolved minerals. Houston water carries enough calcium and magnesium that sediment builds in the bottom of a tank steadily, and everything that goes wrong downstream follows from that.

  • Rumbling or popping when the burner runs is steam escaping from under a sediment layer. It is the classic sign.
  • Slower recovery — hot water running out sooner than it used to — is sediment insulating the burner from the water.
  • Higher gas or electricity use for the same hot water, same cause.
  • Premature tank failure, because sediment holds heat against the tank bottom and accelerates corrosion.

Flushing the tank once a year is the single highest-value maintenance task on a Houston water heater, and almost nobody does it. A tank flushed annually will typically outlast one that never is by several years.

The anode rod nobody mentions

Inside every tank water heater is a sacrificial anode rod — a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not. It is consumable. When it is gone, the tank starts rusting from the inside.

Replacing an anode rod costs a fraction of a new water heater, and on a tank that is otherwise healthy at six or seven years it can add years of life. It is almost never offered, because it is a small job with a small invoice. Ask about it.

Repair or replace

Tank water heaters generally last eight to twelve years, and hard water pushes that toward the lower end.

  • Under eight years: repair is usually right. Elements, thermostats, valves and anode rods are all straightforward.
  • Eight to twelve: depends on the repair. A thermostat, yes. A leaking tank, no.
  • A leaking tank at any age is replacement. The tank cannot be repaired, and a slow leak becomes a fast one without warning.

Worth knowing before you call

  • Gas or electric, and how old — the date is on the serial number label.
  • Tank capacity in gallons.
  • Whether the water is discoloured.
  • Whether there is water on the floor, and whether it is at the base or at a fitting above.
  • Whether it has ever been flushed.
